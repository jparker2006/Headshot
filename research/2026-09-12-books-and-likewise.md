## 1. Likewise / Pix — **still operating, but sold and pivoted away from the app**

### What it is
A Seattle-area entertainment recommendation service covering TV, movies, books and podcasts. Founded **2017** by former Microsoft communications chief **Larry Cohen**, spun out of Bill Gates' private office. CEO through the Pix era was **Ian Morris**. Source: [TechCrunch, 5 Oct 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/10/05/likewise-debuts-pix-an-ai-chatbot-for-entertainment-recommendations/).

### Ownership / funding
- Backed by **Bill Gates / Gates Frontier**. Reported figure: **$15M from Gates** ([Techopedia](https://www.techopedia.com/why-did-bill-gates-invest-15m-in-ai-assistant-startup-likewise)). A full round-by-round table was not obtainable — Crunchbase returned 403.
- **4 June 2025: acquired by Watchlist Inc.**, a newly formed holding company founded by **Josh Epstein** (previously Likewise's Chief Business Officer), who became CEO. **Gates Frontier retained a minority interest.** Primary source: [PR Newswire, 4 June 2025](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/likewise-announces-acquisition-by-watchlist-inc-betting-big-on-the-future-of-newsletters-and-personalized-entertainment-discovery-302473317.html); confirmed by [Publishers Weekly, 5 June 2025](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/industry-deals/article/97948-likewise-gets-new-owners-targets-book-discovery-market.html) and [MergerLinks](https://app.mergerlinks.com/transactions/2025-06-04-likewise/dealmakers) (described as a majority stake bought from Gates Frontier). Terms undisclosed.
- Investors/advisors joining at acquisition: **Bob Bakish** (former Paramount CEO, advisor), board chair **Mino Capossela** (ex-Goldman Sachs partner), plus executives from Google, YouTube, Reddit, AppNexus, Comcast, TelevisaUnivision (PW, 5 Jun 2025).

### Traction
- **6M+ registered users, ~2M monthly actives** — as of Oct 2023 (TechCrunch, 5 Oct 2023).
- **8.4M active newsletter subscriptions** across its three flagship newsletters (Watchlist = TV/movies, Booklist = books, Podlist = podcasts), **+300% YoY audience growth, revenue doubled in each of the prior two years** — as of June 2025 (PR Newswire / PW). No post-2025 user number is public.
- App store: iOS "Likewise: Movie, TV, Book Recs" — **4.7★, 47K ratings**, seller Likewise, Inc. ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/likewise-movie-tv-book-recs/id1264195462)).

### The critical signal
**`likewise.com` now 301-redirects to `pix-media.com`** (verified by fetch, Sept 2026). [pix-media.com](https://pix-media.com/) is a stripped-down **newsletter signup + "Compose an Ask"** page — no about page, no team, no app marketing, no mention of Likewise or Gates. And the iOS app's **last update was version 32.37 on 1 September 2025** — roughly a year stale as of today. The company is alive; the *app* is effectively in maintenance while the business runs on newsletters. Epstein's own framing: newsletters are "the new direct-to-consumer front door to media" (PR Newswire, 4 Jun 2025).

### AI features and dates
- **Pix launched 5 October 2023** — an entertainment chatbot built on **OpenAI GPT-3.5/4** plus Likewise's ~**600 million consumer data points**. Reachable via SMS (877-TEXTPIX), email (pix@likewise.com), web, the mobile app, and a **TV app with voice-remote input**. Free; ad-supported. (TechCrunch, 5 Oct 2023; [PR Newswire, 5 Oct 2023](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/likewise-introduces-pix---the-worlds-first-personal-entertainment-companion-301948431.html).)
- **March 2024**: AI-powered mobile app upgrades, Pix embedded deeper into all searching/browsing/recommendations ([PR Newswire, 21 Mar 2024](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/entertainment-discovery-platform-likewise-introduces-new-ai-powered-mobile-app-upgrades-to-its-content-recommendations-ecosystem-302087504.html)).
- **On your "Pix chatbot on Bing" hypothesis: I could not verify any Bing integration.** No source I fetched mentions Bing, Microsoft distribution, or a Bing-hosted Pix. Treat that as unconfirmed. The Microsoft link is personnel/capital (Gates, ex-Microsoft founder), not product.

### MULTIPLAYER? — **Yes, but weakly, and it's been deprioritized**
The App Store description sells a real social layer: *"Enthusiasts come together on Likewise. Browse and share recommendations and connect with like-minded people in groups you're interested in. Invite your friends and family and never miss what they're enjoying."* Concretely: **crowd-sourced recommendations, interest-based groups, posting and commenting on titles, shareable lists, friend/family invites**. Reviewers complain about a gate where you must "like" an item before commenting.

But the social graph is not the business. The acquisition thesis is **one-to-many broadcast (newsletters)**, not friend-to-friend. It is best read as *community-flavoured single-player*: your taste profile + a public community feed, not a mutual-friends graph. Notably, users repeatedly criticise the recommendation quality — "the algorithm is mediocre at best," "recommendations engine is somewhere between non-existent and bare bones" (App Store reviews).

## 2. Goodreads — **the stagnant incumbent; no AI shipped**

### What it is
The world's largest book cataloguing and review social network. Founded **December 2006** by **Otis Chandler and Elizabeth Khuri Chandler**, launched **January 2007**. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodreads); [Goodreads About](https://www.goodreads.com/about/us).)

### Ownership
**Acquired by Amazon, announced 28 March 2013, undisclosed sum.** The Authors Guild called it "a truly devastating act of vertical integration" (Wikipedia, citing 2013 coverage). Still an Amazon company — the site footer reads "An Amazon Company."

### Traction numbers over time
From the Wikipedia growth timeline (each figure company-reported at the time):
- **2007**: 650,000 members
- **2012**: 10M members, 20M monthly visits
- **July 2013** (post-acquisition): 20M members
- **2023 onward**: **"over 150 million members"** — repeated in [TechXplore/Guardian syndication, Feb 2025](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-02-amazon-goodreads-community-division-rival.html) ("approximately 150 million users") and still cited in [March 2026 reviews](https://bookwiseapp.com/blog/goodreads-review-is-it-still-worth-using-in-2026).

**Important caveat:** the **current official About page no longer states a member count** (verified by fetch, Sept 2026). It now leads with a different stat: the recommendation engine analyses **"20 billion data points."** The 90M and 150M figures are historical company claims, not freshly confirmed 2026 numbers, and "members" means registered accounts, not actives — Goodreads has never published MAU.

### AI features (2024–2026)
**None that I could verify.** The Goodreads blog (fetched Sept 2026) is entirely editorial — "Readers' 60 Most Anticipated Fall Books," "120 Books to Celebrate 30 Years of Oprah's Book Club," "2026's Dark Academia and Campus Novels" — with no product/AI announcements. Independent 2026 assessment: *"Goodreads has no AI reading companion, no meaningful reading statistics, and no tools that help you understand your reading habits over time"* ([Bookwise, 4 Mar 2026](https://bookwiseapp.com/blog/goodreads-review-is-it-still-worth-using-in-2026)). Note that Amazon's AI book work (Kindle AI recaps) sits in Kindle, not Goodreads.

### The stagnation complaints (well documented)
- **API shutdown, December 2020**: Goodreads deactivated all API keys older than 30 days and stopped issuing new ones — killing the third-party ecosystem (Wikipedia).
- **UI rot**: *"Goodreads' UI hasn't been substantially redesigned since before its Amazon acquisition. It looks and feels like a product from 2010"* (Bookwise, Mar 2026). The Guardian/TechXplore piece calls it "rather clunky" after years without significant updates (Feb 2025).
- **Review bombing and harassment**: unverified accounts can rate books before publication; "weaponized" reviews, extortion attempts and coordinated campaigns disproportionately target authors of colour and LGBTQI+ writers, with moderation widely judged insufficient (Wikipedia; TechXplore Feb 2025).
- **Iran account suspensions** in 2019 and again in **2025**, citing sanctions and export-control rules (Wikipedia).

### 2025–2026 redesign
**July 2025: Goodreads changed its logo for the first time in ~20 years** — a softer, chunkier serif wordmark, refreshed illustrations, and seasonal reading-challenge badges. I could not fetch the primary Goodreads announcement (Medium analysis returned 403, Book Riot 404), so treat this as a single-sourced secondary claim. **It was a brand refresh, not a product rebuild** — the consensus in the 2026 coverage is that the underlying product did not change.

### MULTIPLAYER? — **Yes, fully. This is its moat and its liability.**
Goodreads is the canonical multiplayer book product: **friends and followers, a friends' activity feed, public reviews with threaded comments, groups/forums, buddy reads, the annual Reading Challenge, the Goodreads Choice Awards (launched 2009, community-voted), and "My Year in Books" (created 2014)**. Every review is a public, commentable object. That open commenting is precisely what makes review bombing and pile-ons possible — and it is the single design decision StoryGraph deliberately reversed.

## 3. The StoryGraph — **bootstrapped, growing fast, deliberately low-social**

### What it is
An independent, Amazon-free Goodreads alternative built around **mood- and pace-based** book tracking, deep reading statistics, and content warnings. Created **2019** by London software engineer **Nadia Odunayo**, initially as a side project. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_StoryGraph); [We Are Founders profile](https://www.wearefounders.uk/the-storygraph-how-nadia-odunayo-built-a-better-alternative-to-goodreads/).)

### Ownership / funding — **bootstrapped, no VC**
Explicitly **not venture-backed**, grown by word of mouth (We Are Founders). Odunayo has publicly ruled out selling to Amazon ([Startup Ecosystem Canada](https://www.startupecosystem.ca/news/nadia-odunayo-challenges-amazons-goodreads-with-the-storygraph/), summarising the Guardian interview). **No funding rounds exist to report.**

### Traction (with dates)
- **3.8M active users** — Feb 2025, from David Barnett's Guardian interview with Odunayo, 17 Feb 2025 ([Techmeme index](https://www.techmeme.com/250217/p3); [TechXplore syndication](https://techxplore.com/news/2025-02-amazon-goodreads-community-division-rival.html)).
- **~4M signups** — April 2025 (Wikipedia).
- **5M signups passed** — **January 2026** (Wikipedia). So roughly **1.2M net new signups in ~11 months**.
- Growth is spiky and politically driven: after the **2024 US elections**, up to **~25,000 new signups in a single day** (Wikipedia / search summary).
- **2025 App Store Awards**: the iOS app was a recipient; Apple Editors' Choice badge on the listing; currently **#21 in Books**, **4.5★ from 3.6K ratings** ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/the-storygraph/id1570489264)).
- **Revenue is not public.** Team size is not public. Do not guess.

### Revenue model — StoryGraph Plus
**$4.99/month or $49.99/year** (App Store listing, Sept 2026; Wikipedia recorded $50/yr as of 2023 — pricing has been stable). Plus buys **custom charts, advanced stats filtering, roadmap voting, and priority support**. The core product, including all social features, is free. No ads.

### AI / algorithm angle — and the stance
- The recommendation engine is **machine learning over your own reading history and preference surveys**, not bestseller lists; the App Store listing markets "AI recommendations." The Guardian/TechXplore piece notes StoryGraph "integrates machine learning for personalized recommendations and automated summaries" (Feb 2025).
- **On generative AI, the honest answer is: their public roadmap contains zero AI features.** I fetched [roadmap.thestorygraph.com](https://roadmap.thestorygraph.com/) (1,787 tracked feature requests, 323 bugs) and found **no AI items at all** — the shipping priorities are social and organisational. thestorygraph.com itself returns 403 to fetchers, so I could not retrieve a formal written AI policy; **I did not find an explicit anti-AI manifesto and will not invent one.** The accurate characterisation is: ML-for-recommendations yes, generative-AI product surface no, and no generative AI on the roadmap.

### MULTIPLAYER? — **Yes, but deliberately throttled. This is the most interesting design decision of the four.**
What exists (App Store listing + Wikipedia + roadmap):
- **Friends**, follow relationships, friends' reading-activity feed
- **Buddy reads**
- **Book clubs**, public and private
- **Community reading challenges**, giveaways, reading goals
- Profile privacy: public / members-only / private
- **Liking reviews shipped February 2026**

What is deliberately absent:
- **No DMs.** Called out in the Guardian headline itself (17 Feb 2025).
- **No comments on reviews.** Odunayo's stated reasoning: she wanted to *"avoid a situation where anyone can just comment on your review and you've got to deal with being scared to put reviews up there"* (Guardian via TechXplore, Feb 2025). Wikipedia confirms users can like reviews but cannot comment on them.

So: **an asymmetric, low-conflict social graph** — you can follow, read alongside, and join clubs, but you cannot reply to a stranger. It is multiplayer for co-reading, single-player for opinion. Both "comment on friends' reading activity" (in progress) and "find/invite your real-life friends to StoryGraph" (medium-term) are still *open roadmap items* — meaning friend discovery is still a known weakness in 2026. Also on the roadmap: **Book Clubs V2** (96 votes, ideas stage) and a **Kobo × StoryGraph partnership shipping May 2026**.

## 4. Fable (fable.co) — **acquired, AI-burned, and yes: it does TV and film**

### What it is
A social reading app — "for bookworms and bingewatchers" — built around **clubs**: influencer- and celebrity-hosted reading groups with spoiler-protected discussion rooms, milestones, lists, stats, streaks, and in-app ebook purchase/reading. Founded **2019** by **Padmasree Warrior** (ex-CTO of Cisco and Motorola, ex-CEO of NIO USA, board member at Microsoft and Spotify). ([fable.co](https://fable.co/); [Publishers Weekly, 11 Jan 2022](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industry-news/industry-deals/article/88260-fable-raises-20-million-partners-with-bookstore.html).)

> ⚠️ **Name collision warning — three unrelated "Fables" pollute every search.** (a) **fable.co** — the book/TV social app, this report's subject. (b) **Fable Studio** — the AI company behind **Showrunner**, the "Netflix of AI," which took an **Amazon Alexa Fund investment in 2025** ([Variety, 2025](https://variety.com/2025/digital/news/netflix-of-ai-amazon-invests-fable-showrunner-launch-1236471989/)). (c) **Fable Tech Labs** — accessibility, and **fable.app** — a motion-design tool that raised **$15M Series A led by Redpoint in Dec 2021**. Tracxn and PitchBook conflate these; I discarded Tracxn's "Amazon invested July 2025" and "Toan Dinh CEO Sept 2025" entries as belonging to the other companies.

### Ownership / funding
- **Seed: ~$7.25M** (Redpoint Ventures led; Tracxn dates a ~$6.31M seed to 3 Aug 2020).
- **Series A: $20M, announced 11 January 2022**, led by **Tiger Global**, with **Redpoint Ventures, Breyer Capital, defy.vc, Gaingels**. (Publishers Weekly, 11 Jan 2022; [The Story Exchange](https://thestoryexchange.org/padmasree-warrior-book-club-startup-fable-raises-200-million/).)
- **Total raised: ~$27.25–28.8M** depending on source rounding.
- **ACQUIRED: Fable joined Everand (a Scribd, Inc. company) on 9 June 2025.** Primary source: [Scribd Inc. newsroom, "Fable joins Everand"](https://www.scribdinc.com/newsroom/fable-joins-everand). Confirmed on [Scribd's About page](https://www.scribdinc.com/about): *"Padma Warrior is the founder of Fable, which was acquired by Scribd, Inc. in 2025."* Terms undisclosed. **All Fable employees transferred to Scribd Inc.; Warrior moved to an advisory role; Tony Grimminck remains Scribd Inc. CEO.** Fable is now one of four Scribd brands alongside Scribd, Everand and Slideshare.
- Post-acquisition Scribd Inc. scale: **250M+ monthly unique visitors, 2M+ paying subscribers, 30M+ hours read/month, 400M+ pieces of content**; the newsroom notes Scribd Inc. **surpassed one billion user accounts in 2026**. (Scribd About / newsroom, fetched Sept 2026.)
- **No new funding round for fable.co after the acquisition** — it no longer raises independently. Any "Fable raises in 2025/2026" headline is one of the other two companies.

### Traction numbers
- **300+ public book clubs**, 25 employees, largest club "Spicy Romance Book Club" at **11,192 members** — Jan 2022 (PW).
- **50,000+ online communities for books and TV shows; 1M+ books in catalogue** — Oct 2024 ([PBS, 31 Oct 2024](https://www.pbs.org/about/about-pbs/blogs/news/new-pbs-film-club-launches-first-branded-community-on-fable-for-both-book-and-tv-fans/)).
- **~2 million users** received the AI year-end summaries — Fable's own statement, 2 Jan 2025 ([Smart Bitches Trashy Books, Jan 2025](https://smartbitchestrashybooks.com/2025/01/fables-reader-summary-features-racism-and-probably-ai/)). This is the closest thing to a public user count.
- **~100,000 active book clubs** — June 2025, at acquisition (Scribd newsroom).
- Current app homepage shows clubs in the low thousands of members each: "Books & Coffee Life" 3.4k, "Book Babes" 3.3k (fable.co, Sept 2026).

### TV AND MOVIES — **CONFIRMED, and here is the date**
**Fable launched TV Clubs on 23 April 2024.** Primary source: Fable's own blog post, ["Our story world just got bigger: welcome home, bookworms and binge-watchers"](https://fable.co/blog/our-story-world-just-got-bigger-welcome-home-bookworms-and-binge-watchers), dated 23 April 2024, and the help article ["Why is Fable expanding to TV Shows?"](https://help.fable.co/article/114-why-is-fable-expanding-to-tv-shows), last updated the same day.

What shipped:
- **TV clubs** — *"You'll find TV clubs to watch and discuss specific shows or genres of shows, just like our book clubs"*
- **Episode-level discussion rooms with spoiler protection**
- **TV tracking lists**: want to watch / currently watching / finished / didn't finish — i.e. a full watch-log, directly parallel to their reading log
- **TV content in the For You feed** with trailers, images, articles
- **A content-preference toggle**: users control whether they see *just books, just TV, or a mix of both* — books remain default-prioritised ("Books are built into our DNA")

**Film specifically**: the launch included creator-run film clubs (e.g. "Zai Rambles' Film Club"), and **PBS launched PBS FILM CLUB on Fable on 31 October 2024** — Fable's *first branded community serving both book and TV fans*, alongside TV creators like Mammoth Mafia and Jess Spoll. The help centre still lists "change my TV Show and Books content preferences" as a live setting in 2026. So: **TV is a first-class object; film exists via clubs but the product taxonomy is "Books and TV Shows."**

### The AI controversy — full timeline
Fable used the **OpenAI API** to generate playful year-end "reader summaries" in **December 2024**. They produced bigoted output about race, sexuality, disability and gender.

- **30 Dec 2024** — user @tianas_littalk (book influencer **Tiana Trammell**) posts her summary: *"Soulful Explorer: Your journey dives deep into the heart of Black narratives and transformative tales, leaving mainstream stories gasping for air. Don't forget to surface for the occasional white author, okay?"* ([Daily Dot](https://www.dailydot.com/news/fable-app-racist-ai/); Smart Bitches.)
- A second widely shared one: *"Diversity Devotee: Your bookshelf is a vibrant kaleidoscope of voices and experiences making me wonder if you're ever in the mood for a straight, cis white man's perspective!"* Writer **Danny Groves** was among those affected; readers also reported summaries with offensive framing around **disability** and **sexual orientation**. ([Lit Hub](https://lithub.com/fables-ai-generated-end-of-year-reading-summaries-veered-into-bigotry/); [AI Incident Database #882](https://incidentdatabase.ai/cite/882/), incident date 29 Dec 2024.)
- **31 Dec 2024** — Fable responds publicly: "this one is not ok."
- **1 Jan 2025** — Fable apologises, states summaries should never reference *"race, sexuality, religion, nationality, or other protected classes."*
- **2 Jan 2025** — **Chris Gallello, Fable's head of product**, posts a video apology acknowledging reports of the feature using *"very bigoted, racist language."* His stated remediation: **label summaries as AI-generated, add an opt-out, strip the "playfulness" from the prompt, add feedback buttons** — explicitly **not** removing AI. This landed badly; many users deleted accounts, one writing that Fable's *"commitment is to AI usage rather than ensuring safety of their users."* (Daily Dot; Smart Bitches.)

**Did they kill the feature?** — **Yes, ultimately, after the Jan 2 half-measure failed.** Fable reversed within days and **committed to removing the AI-powered features from the app** (Lit Hub: "The company quickly disabled the feature… committed to removing all AI-powered features from the app"; AI Incident Database #882 records the resolution as *"Fable will remove its popular AI features,"* citing Wired, USA Today and NYT coverage). **Caveat: I could not fetch the Wired/TechCrunch primaries directly** (Wired blocks fetching; the TechCrunch URL 404'd), so the removal rests on Lit Hub + the incident database's citation of Wired. The two-step — *guardrails first, removal only after backlash* — is the part worth internalising.

Corroborating the removal: the pre-controversy AI agent **"Scout"** — described by PBS in Oct 2024 as *"an AI-powered recommendation agent… that delivers hyper-personalized suggestions based on tropes and specific criteria"* — **no longer appears anywhere**: not in the Fable help centre (which has zero AI articles as of Sept 2026 — no Scout, no reader summaries), and not on fable.co. The current homepage markets **"personalized reader summaries"** as stats-based genre breakdowns and monthly recaps, with no AI framing. Fable went from AI-forward to AI-silent in eighteen months.

### 2025–2026 status
Operating and current: footer reads **"©2026 Fable Group Inc."**; the blog is publishing weekly through **August–September 2026** (New Release Radar, Hispanic Heritage Month lists, genre guides), now with **blended Fable and Everand editorial branding**. Goodreads import, browser extension (beta), Fable Plus subscription (bundling Everand audiobooks/documents), individual ebook purchases all live.

### MULTIPLAYER? — **Yes. The most aggressively multiplayer of the four; clubs are the entire product.**
Fable is not a log with a social skin — it is a **club-first** product where the group is the primary object:
- **Clubs** for books *and* TV shows, public or private, with **multiple moderators**, member management and privacy settings
- **Discussion rooms with spoiler protection**, scoped per book and **per TV episode**
- **Milestones** — the club reads to a checkpoint together, gating discussion (the mechanic that makes async co-reading work)
- **Host-led / celebrity- and influencer-run clubs** as the acquisition channel: LeVar Burton, Sean Astin, and branded clubs like **PBS FILM CLUB** (Oct 2024)
- **"Social Mode"**, public/private lists, folios, a social feed, Goodreads import for graph portability
- Club continuity across titles — the help centre addresses *"What happens to discussions once we switch books or TV shows?"*

The distinctive mechanic versus everyone else: **milestone-gated, spoiler-protected group discussion**, which is exactly the primitive TV needs and which Goodreads and StoryGraph both lack.

## Cross-cutting read for a social movie/TV taste app

**1. The multiplayer spectrum is the real axis.** Fable = clubs-first, group is the primary object. Goodreads = full open social graph, and its open commenting is the direct cause of its worst problem (review bombing). StoryGraph = deliberately *subtractive* social — friends, buddy reads and clubs, but **no DMs and no review comments**, and it is winning share precisely on that restraint. Likewise = community feed + groups, but the social graph was never the business and the acquirer replaced it with newsletters.

**2. The books world has already run the "AI-generated year-in-review" experiment, and it went badly.** Fable's Dec 2024 recaps are the single most instructive artefact here: a *playful, roasting* tone applied by an LLM to a user's identity-adjacent consumption history produced bigoted output at ~2M-user scale, and the company ended up **retreating from AI entirely**. If you ship personality-driven AI commentary on what someone watches, the failure mode is not "wrong recommendation," it is "your app called me out for watching Black films." The lesson from their timeline: their first response (guardrails, opt-out, less playfulness) was rejected — only full removal ended it.

**3. Deterministic-fact grounding is a genuine differentiator.** Goodreads has shipped *no* AI and is criticised for it; StoryGraph has *no generative AI on a 1,787-item roadmap*; Fable *withdrew* its AI. Nobody in the closest analogue market has a credible AI layer right now. A system whose claims are computed rather than generated — your `graphReason` rule, "no reason, no pick" — is the direct answer to the Fable failure, and it is currently unoccupied ground.

**4. Consolidation is the 2025 story.** Both VC-backed players in this space were absorbed within five days of each other: Likewise → Watchlist Inc. (4 Jun 2025), Fable → Everand/Scribd (9 Jun 2025). The only one growing on its own terms is the **bootstrapped, no-VC, subscription-funded** one — StoryGraph, at $4.99/mo, 3.8M actives (Feb 2025) → 5M signups (Jan 2026), with an Apple App Store Award in 2025.
