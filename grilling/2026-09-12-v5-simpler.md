## Claude's position (v5)

The product has three verbs and the UI should have exactly three places: **Log** (what did you watch, how good was it), **Ask** (talk to the thing that has read your log), **You** (what it knows: profile, numbers, your list). Everything else is a setting. v4 blurred Ask into You with one prompt that does two jobs; that is the confusion. Split them.

The sidebar is the Glox shape: a short fixed list of sections on top (Log, Ask, You), and, only when you are in Ask, the chat sessions below it. Sections are not sessions; sessions are not sections. Ask looks like ChatGPT/Claude: centered column, message rows, a big composer that is obviously a chat composer. The You page stops trying to be a dashboard: profile prose, a few numbers, the list.

Ratings: steal IMDb. 1–10 is one tap on a row of ten, maps cleanly to liked/meh (≥7 / <7) for the engine and to Letterboxd/IMDb imports without loss, and it is what a film buff already thinks in. Stars are 1–10 with a worse keyboard story. Keep "want" and "seen" as states, not scores.

Swiping is "recreate your history": a standalone Log feature ("Catch up"), not onboarding. Onboarding is: sign in, pick services, catch up if you want. Imports and services move to Settings, reached from the account menu.

## Round 2 (what the website is; what gets cut)

Two things to chase. A6 says assume every user connects Letterboxd, but session 4 decided no crawl, CSV export only; a CSV export is a five-step trip through Letterboxd settings on a laptop and kills "under a minute". And A6's "have it start to rank things it's seen": after a Letterboxd import the ratings are already there, so the thing to rate is what it *guesses* you've seen (the deck). Q3 and Q4 pin both.

**Q1. One sentence.** Rec: "Headshot is a log of everything you've watched that can talk." That's the website. The log is the product, the chat is the interface to it, the profile is the receipt. If a feature isn't the log, the chat, or the profile, it goes.
**Q2. The cut list.** Rec: cut the nightly cron and the stored Tonight (the pick becomes an on-demand chat answer), cut Later as a page (it's a filter on the log: rating "want"), cut the Numbers page as a separate thing (the numbers live on You as one sentence and two rows), cut the taste map code path, cut the public answer pages' follow-up chips, cut `/brain`. Keep: log, 1–10, deck as catch-up, chat with memory, profile, imports, services, drawer.
**Q3. Letterboxd in under a minute.** Rec: bring back the username crawl for the *public* profile (films, ratings, watchlist are public by default on Letterboxd; no login, no password); the CSV upload stays as the fallback for private profiles. Onboarding asks for the username, imports in the background, and moves on.
**Q4. What "rate things it's seen" means.** Rec: after the import, the catch-up deck shows the ten titles it most suspects you've seen but that aren't in your Letterboxd (neighbours of your top-rated people); each card is "seen it? rate 1–10 / not seen". That's the minute. No deck at all if you skipped Letterboxd; it's offered on Log.
**Q5. Where tonight lives.** Rec: a new chat opens with one line from the agent: "tonight: X on Max, because …" with a Play link, computed on demand from the existing engine (no cron, no stored rows). Asking "what should I watch" gives the same thing with filters.
**Q6. Memory visible.** Rec: You opens with the profile prose (the memory) at the top, editable, with "rewritten 3m ago"; the chat's receipts say "from your profile" when it used it. Nothing else changes about memory.
**Q7. The drawer.** Rec: stays as the one way to look at a title or person; gains the 1–10 row; loses the bucket words.
**Q8. Sidebar, concretely.** Rec: 232px like Glox: wordmark; Log / Ask / You with icons; under Ask, when active, "new chat" and the sessions; footer = account (name, Settings, Sign out). Phone: bottom tab bar with the three, sessions inside Ask.
**Q9. Migration of ratings.** Rec: existing liked → 8, meh → 5, want and seen unchanged; IMDb import keeps its number; Letterboxd stars ×2; the engine keeps working off liked = ≥7. One migration, reversible.
**Q10. What ships first.** Rec: (A) research + two boards (shell, Ask) and the 1–10 model + migration; (B) Log page with catch-up and the drawer; (C) Ask as a real chat with tonight as the opener; (D) You + Settings + onboarding with the username crawl, and the cut list executed.

## Round 3 (the hard parts)

**Q1. The crawl, concretely.** Rec: server-side fetch of `letterboxd.com/<user>/films/` pages (poster grid with rating classes), `/watchlist/`, and `/films/diary/` for dates, paginated, polite (one request at a time, 300ms apart, a real User-Agent, cached per user for a day); a profile set to private returns nothing and the UI says so and offers the CSV. If Letterboxd blocks the crawl at some point, the CSV path is already there. Films are matched by the Letterboxd slug + year against the graph, same matcher as the CSV.
**Q2. Rating storage.** Rec: `watched.rating` stays the text state column (liked / meh / want / seen / unseen are replaced by `rated` / `want` / `seen` / `unseen`) and a new integer column `score` 1–10 nullable; `liked` in every engine/taste query becomes `score >= 7`, `meh` becomes `score <= 6`. The migration fills `score` from the old bucket (liked 8, meh 5) and rewrites `rating` to `rated`. Numbers, memory facts and tests move to the score.
**Q3. What the chat can do, and only that.** Rec: the tool set becomes: log (search + rate + note), my_profile (memory + facts), recall (episodes), have_i_seen, tonight (on-demand pick with filters), and the v2 trivia tools (path, costars, known-for, identify). Nothing else. No mark_progress tool; season progress is an edit on the row from the drawer.
**Q4. Sessions storage.** Rec: chat sessions move server-side (table `threads`: id, user id, title, created, updated; `messages`: thread id, role, parts JSON) for signed-in users so history survives browsers, matching the ChatGPT shape; signed-out trivia stays in localStorage and is not listed. This reverses the "conversations never stored" rule for signed-in users; the CLAUDE.md rule is updated.
**Q5. Signed-out.** Rec: signed-out gets Ask only (trivia, no memory), with the sidebar showing Ask and a Sign in row; Log and You are behind sign-in. The /a permalinks stay.
**Q6. Settings page.** Rec: one page at /settings: account (email, sign out, delete account), services checklist, imports (Letterboxd username or CSV, Netflix CSV, IMDb CSV), and the profile's pinned notes. Nothing else.
**Q7. The catch-up deck source.** Rec: same source as v4's lore deck but scored by "probably seen" (neighbours of your 8+ people, weighted by votes, i.e. famous things near your favourites), ten at a time, each card "seen it → 1–10" or "not seen"; after each ten the memory rewrites and the diff shows. Undo stays.
**Q8. Delete before build.** Rec: remove in code, not hide: cron route + vercel.json schedule, `tonight` table and store methods, TonightPanel, LaterPanel, taste map, /brain page and BrainExplorer, the bucket words UI, the onboarding-inside-You flow, Numbers charts beyond the two rows. Tests for removed things go too.
**Q9. Build order and verification.** Rec: (A) research note + shell and Ask boards → Jake picks or approves; then the score migration and engine/tests. (B) Log page (search, 1–10, note, table, catch-up) + drawer. (C) Ask: sidebar sessions, server-side threads, tonight opener, tool set trimmed. (D) You + Settings + onboarding with the crawl + the delete list. Each chunk verified on production in the browser pane signed in as Jake.
**Q10. Success check at the end.** Rec: a friend with a Letterboxd account signs in, types their username, rates ten cards, and asks "what should I watch tonight" — under two minutes, no explanation from you. That's the v5 done-criterion.

Jake: "Rec is good for all of them, write the spec." Round 3 Q1–Q10 are decisions.

## Decisions (v5)

1. One sentence: Headshot is a log of everything you've watched that can talk. Log is the product, chat is the interface, profile is the receipt.
2. Three sections only: Log, Ask, You. Settings under the account menu. Log is the signed-in landing.
3. Ask is a real chat in the ChatGPT/Claude shape; sessions listed under the Ask section in the Glox-style sidebar; server-side threads for signed-in users (rule change); signed-out gets trivia-only Ask.
4. Ratings are 1–10 (IMDb style) with states rated / want / seen / unseen; score ≥7 is "liked" everywhere; migration liked→8, meh→5.
5. Log page: search, 1–10, note, the log table; Catch-up (the deck) is a button there, never onboarding. Deck ranked by "probably seen", ten at a time, memory diff after each ten.
6. You: memory prose on top (editable, visible), numbers as one sentence + people row + decades row, the Later list. Tonight becomes the opening line of a new chat, computed on demand; no cron, no stored Tonight.
7. Onboarding: sign in → services → Letterboxd username (public-profile crawl; CSV fallback) → rate ten cards → Log. Under a minute.
8. Settings: account, services, imports, pinned notes. Nothing else.
9. Chat tools: log, my_profile, recall, have_i_seen, tonight, trivia tools. Nothing else.
10. Delete in code: cron, tonight table, Tonight/Later panels, taste map, /brain, bucket words, onboarding-inside-You, Numbers charts beyond two rows.
11. Build order A–D as in round 3 Q9; done when a Letterboxd-owning friend gets from sign-in to a tonight pick in under two minutes unaided.
